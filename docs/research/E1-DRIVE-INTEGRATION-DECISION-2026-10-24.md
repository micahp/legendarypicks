## Physics findings: one narrow experiment, not a new blocking workstream

- Week 1–5 tracking has ~10-Hz x/y ball motion, **no ball height or foot positions**. The reported engine lacks goalpost colliders, carrier-fumble/recovery transitions, and foot-based inbounds catch adjudication. It uses approximate ground-bounce dynamics and 2D player-disc impulses. The 176-play test (122/124 end spots within tolerance, one **3.65-yard** OOB spot miss) is useful regression evidence, **not accurate NFL contact/catch/ball-trajectory validation**.
- Canonical movement cohort remains **9,289 plays** (weeks 1–5), with reserved weeks 6–9 and the frozen 49-row manifest untouched. Block assignment separation (median 1.14–1.30 m with 93% hitting minimum at the final frame) and credited tackle-carrier separation (median 0.671 m) are **geometry/proxy** measurements, not proof of physical collision correctness. No new elaborate collision policy justified.
- **Approved only as separate, research-only investigation:** Compare current OOB **center > 0.3-yard threshold** against a predeclared body-edge/proxy rule on the **same 260 observed OOB-worded runs**, stratifying 244 continuous tracks where applicable. Record event timing differences, sideline spot error, false early calls and any new failures on **in-bounds negative controls**. Tracking lacks toes, so do not call body-edge the legal truth. Freeze baseline/metric definitions **before** candidate tuning, respect independent game-clustered evaluation and preserve the unused weeks 6–9 holdout. No solver rewrite, no production switch and **do not block E2 on this experiment**.
- Other missing physical features (post strikes, height-verified bounces, foot placement, fumbles) remain clearly labeled **unsupported**. The next simulation milestone is a coherent **full game state** first, not a fabricated claim that those physics are already accurate.
