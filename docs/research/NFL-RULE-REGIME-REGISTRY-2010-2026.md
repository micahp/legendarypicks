# NFL annual playing-rule changes, 2010–2026

**Status: Expanded, sourced annual inventory; not certified as a full line-by-line difference of 17 official rulebook editions.**

**Records:** 124. **Years:** 17. **Structured data:** [NFL-RULE-REGIME-REGISTRY-2010-2026.json](NFL-RULE-REGIME-REGISTRY-2010-2026.json).

## Scope and classification

Each year's section below lists individual adopted playing-rule changes or distinct source-backed enforcement/eligibility transitions. The data separately flags **temporary expiries**, **competition-format changes**, and **conditional 2026 provisions**. It does **not** conflate rejected proposals, operating bylaws, or NFL statistical-provider data changes with on-field playing-rule amendments.

**Important limits:** Contemporary approved-rule releases and official printed summary-of-changes tables are not the same as a machine-verified textual difference of all old and new Rulebook articles. The earliest years (2010–2013 and 2015) rely partly on contemporary club/league reporting rather than a retrieved full amendment matrix. This document should not be used to assert that every incidental in-season approved ruling, correction, roster bylaw, or rulebook editorial change has been enumerated.

## Machine-use conditions

- Historical PBP records are not transplanted directly between regimes. Attach source season, phase (REG/POST/PRE), source rule IDs, target season and target active-rule IDs.
- Build an active-rule resolver with explicitly bounded eras for overtime, onside, touchbacks, PAT, and replay. A rule being first adopted does not guarantee unchanged application in all later seasons.
- Regime-aware legality first, then empirical distribution modeling: reconstruct eligible **opportunities** (e.g., fourth downs or TD tries) instead of modeling choices from selected actions alone.
- Match raw event, converted event, clock, spot, and possession semantics to the current target rule. Freeze rule selection and hash the registry in every simulation batch.
- **Do not enable the 2026 one-year contingent officiating replacement provision absent a documented trigger.**
- Do not treat 2010–2011 regular-season overtime as if modified sudden death already applied; that era change was postseason-only until 2012.
- Do not treat 2024 onside restrictions, 2025 trailing-only anytime declaration, and 2026 anytime declaration regardless of score as identical.
- The 2015 try change was a trial that was made permanent in 2016; the replay pass-interference trial in 2019 was not renewed in 2020.

## Specific corrections to the prior 39-change inventory

1. **2026 onside:** 2025 allowed an onside declaration at any time only if trailing; the 2026 rule allows one at any time regardless of the score. These are distinct.
2. **2026 contingency:** the separately approved one-year replacement-officials oversight power is contingent on a qualifying work stoppage and must not be modeled as automatically active.
3. **2010–2012 overtime:** the 2010 modified OT change affected postseason, not regular season until 2012.
4. **2015 extra points:** the PAT and defensive conversion changes were adopted as a one-year experiment, then made permanent in 2016.
5. **2018:** the specific turnover-return continuation is a separate overtime article, even where it appears obscure.
6. **Temporary versus permanent:** one-year adoption and renewals are retained as different records, including the 2019 PI-replay trial and 2020 expiry.
