## 1. Bovada's bare coupon path truncates every league

Any query parameter defeats it. Same minute:

```
soccer/north-america/united-states/mls   bare  0 events    lang=en 15
baseball/mlb                             bare 15 events    param   27
```

So MLS read as "Bovada does not carry this league" while it carried the whole
13-fixture slate, and **MLB has been ingesting roughly half its fixtures** for
an unknown period. Not re-measured for the other leagues yet.

Use `lang=en`. I tried `marketFilterId=def` first and it is a trap: it is
Bovada's default market *filter*, returns Game Lines only, and strips every
player prop. Caught before committing, by checking what the parameter returned
rather than assuming a parameter named "default" was neutral.

## 2. MLS was removed on a premise that stopped being true

On 2026-08-17 MLS came out of `LEAGUES` because Bovada prices 2 of the eleven
markets while the relay prices 7, and the stated reason was that a second book
writing goals and assists would mean **two sources disagreeing**.

There was no disagreement to create: the relay writes **zero** goals and zero
assists. Tonight's slate was 428 props across 8 markets, every row
`rotowire:prizepicks`, not one goalscorer line. The newest goals row in the
entire database was 2026-08-17, the day of the removal. The product shipped a
soccer league with no goalscorer market for 19 days.

Restored. Prod now carries goals 1716, assists 390, first_goal_scorer 521,
goal_or_assist 404, on top of the relay's eight. 8 markets to 12, all priced.

## 4. The backoff turned three outages into six

The adaptive backoff rests a league after three empty runs. Truncated runs look
empty, so it slept them: **atp 29 empty runs, wc 63, wta 28, lcup 12, nfl 4**
with the NFL season opening this week. Even after the truncation was fixed the
leagues stayed asleep, and clearing the counters by hand was required.

A backoff that cannot distinguish "the publisher has nothing" from "we asked
wrong" will re-suppress every lane we fix. Unresolved.

## 5. Residential IP is not the lever, and PrizePicks is not the answer

The X outage was solved by a residential pull, so the obvious move was to point
the same machine at PrizePicks. It 403s a **verified residential IP** (Charter,
Austin) using curl with matching headers. Not an IP-range block: a logged-in
browser is served, scripted clients are not. The home-PC session was right to
stop rather than start rotating headers.

And PrizePicks would not have satisfied the requirement anyway. Micah's
requirement is provenance, all the props, **and odds**. PrizePicks props are
**0% priced** — demon and goblin are payout multipliers, not prices. It fills
market coverage and cannot fill the odds column.

# Part 2 (afternoon 14:00 through 05:00 next morning), written 2026-09-06

## The one thing to carry forward, part 2

**The box sat at 100% disk with 0 bytes free for hours while every instrument
read healthy.** Prod answered 200. Timers fired on time. Tests passed. The
morning's lesson was that a 200 with a short list looks identical to an
out-of-season sport; the evening's is that a full disk looks identical to a
working box until you type `df`. Both are the same shape: *nothing raises.*

The second lesson is narrower and sharper. **A gate that is red on every run is
not a gate.** `props-freshness` had been failing every 30 minutes while every
provider it watches was fresh, so its unit state carried no information at all.
If bovada had stopped, nothing would have changed.

## 6. The props board froze the page, and the cap was not the whole story (`3221e55`)

Reported as "several of the league selects are broken" plus an empty state that
flashes before reloading. The previous session found a hard page cap in
`fetchAllProps` that *threw* past 10,000 rows, which produced the "prop board
could not be loaded" banner. That diagnosis was correct and incomplete.

Underneath it, the board fires **one `/api/props/history` request per rendered
row** with nothing bounding the count. NCAAF `receiving_yards` alone is 2,470
props, 687 grouped rows, so the board opened 687 fetches, exhausted the
browser's per-host connection pool and re-rendered the whole list on every
response. Picking a single league did not help, because that 2,470 *is* one
league.

Measured by clicking every select against `:3096`:

| League | before | after | rows | history reqs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | timed out at 6000ms | 806ms | 150 | capped |
| NCAAF | timed out | 699ms | 150 | 150 of 171 |
| NFL | timed out | 889ms | 12 | 12 |
| Soccer | timed out | 350ms | 150 | 150 of 687 |
| Tennis | timed out | 1123ms | 26 | 26 |
| MLB | timed out | 209ms | 150 | 150 of 296 |
| UFC | timed out | 1092ms | 20 | 20 |

Three changes: `fetchAllProps` degrades instead of throwing, a 150-row window
bounds both rendering and the history fan-out with a "Show more", and the window
states itself rather than passing a partial ranking off as a complete one.

**A flaw caught before shipping:** the first version sliced the live hit-rate
sort, so a row returning no history sank out of the window, promoted an unloaded
row in, and walked the whole market a row at a time. Window membership now comes
from the history-independent order and is held fixed.

## 9. THE DISK. 100% full, 0 bytes free, for hours

```
start   391G  372G   0     100%
end     391G  313G   59G    85%   (then 118G free after the WAL)
```

Reclaimed 118 GB total. The breakdown matters because two of the three numbers
Docker reports are misleading:

- **Docker build cache, 51.54 GB.** Reclaimable, zero risk, freed in full.
- **Docker images.** `docker system df` said 25.34 GB reclaimable. Real unique
  bytes were ~11.6 GB, and `docker image prune` (dangling) freed **0 B**,
  because all 10 untagged images shared every layer with a tagged one. Removing
  24 obsolete rollback and candidate tags freed only 3 GB on disk, because 9.29
  GB reappeared as build-cache records that had been accounted inside those
  images; a second `builder prune` took that. Kept the three most recent
  rollback pairs plus `pre-ncaaf-zero-20260830`.
- **One image was 7.2 GB** (`legendarypicks-backend:rollback-pre-v0.7.4`)
  against ~250 MB for its peers. That is the `.dockerignore` trap: a build that
  copied `data/` into the image.

### The 60 GB WAL in front of a 456 MB database

`_kalshi_feed.sqlite3-wal` was **60.4 GB**. The database was 456 MB.

Checkpointing was working the entire time: a PASSIVE checkpoint returned
`busy=0` and copied 13,997,462 of 14,714,496 pages. **SQLite never shrinks a WAL
below its high-water mark.** `kalshi_feed.py:573` prunes aged trades every 60s,
and ~18 `collect_orderbook` / `live_valuefade` readers mean there is never an
instant with zero open snapshots, so the file could never reset. It had been
appending since the feed started.

Only `wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)` reclaims it and it needs every reader detached.
With readers attached it returns `busy=1` and does nothing, safely; that was
verified first. The processes are safe to stop: `watch_live.py` runs from cron
every 4 minutes and respawns them per ticker. In the event the *last process to
detach checkpointed and truncated on close*, so the pause itself was the fix.

Verified across the operation: **zero row delta** (628,444 trades, 310,413
market_state, 148 subscriptions), `integrity_check: ok`, `quick_check: ok`.

Prevented from recurring by `PRAGMA journal_size_limit=268435456` in
`kalshi_feed_store.py` (`05de397`, prediction-market-trading). It was `-1`.

## 10. `props-freshness` was red by construction (`7681e6b`)

38 `UNKNOWN SOURCE` alerts per run across 12 labels, while all three providers
it watches were captured 0.3h earlier. Two different causes:

- **Rotowire's per-book labels** (`rotowire:draftkings-sb`, `:pick6`, `:betr`
  and seven more). The label carries its provider in the prefix, so
  `resolve_provider` reads that encoding instead of enumerating every book
  rotowire invents. A quiet book is absorbed by the provider's own freshness,
  which is the actual question. An unlisted book still prints, as INFO.
- **PrizePicks is not a configured provider at all.** `PROVIDERS` holds bovada,
  underdog, rotowire. `ingest_prizepicks_props.py` takes a json path by hand and
  no timer runs it. Its rows stopped because nobody ran it, so they are
  `RETIRED_SOURCES` alongside kambi.

Proved it can still go red: a 999h-stale provider and an unrecognised vendor
both still exit 1.

`news-x` stays red and should. Every X transport returns nothing and it says so.
The lane has contributed no rows since 2026-08-20.

## 12. The settlement analysis, redone. Two prior claims did not survive

Prod: 105,488 past-dated props, 13,835 unsettled, 12,007 on properly linked
games. The "six times larger than the linking gap" shape held. These did not:

- **"Every prop on an unlinked game is 100% unsettled, without exception."**

- **Void was being counted as a defect.** Void props already have a

pending / errors**. Unmappable and pending both leave no row, so a row-count
measurement cannot tell "we could not map this player" from "we are still
waiting". Those need opposite responses.

## 14. The systemic finding: an ingest existing is not an ingest running

`ingest_soccer_logs.py` is in **no systemd unit, no crontab, no runner script.**
It has only ever run by hand. That single gap is what starves cards, goals and
assists alike.

Freshness is the only honest instrument here. Reachability by name over-counts
(a mention in a comment reads as an edge) and under-counts (runners import
runners):

| league | newest log | newest finaled game | lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| mlb | 2026-09-04 | 2026-09-05 | 1 day |
| lcup | 2026-08-26 | 2026-09-02 | 7 days |
| mls | 2026-08-08 | 2026-09-05 | 28 days |
| ncaaf | 2025-12-13 | 2026-09-05 | 266 days |

MLB is current and is the one league with a dedicated timer. **NCAAF's 266 days
is real but does not block settlement**, which grades off the ESPN boxscore
directly; it starves charts and hit-rate history. 49 `ingest_*.py` are held
together by ~14 timers and a lot of remembering.

The many-ingests design is correct and should not be "simplified": each
publisher is a vocabulary boundary. The unscheduled-ness is the defect.

## 15. UFCStats is prod-only, and its repair tool points backwards

Every other subsystem writes both databases, either as a pair (`news` /
`news-prod`) or in one unit (`legendarypicks-props`, "DEV then PROD").
`legendarypicks-ufc-fight-stats-prod` writes prod only and has **no dev sibling
in `/etc/systemd/system`, none in `ops/systemd/`, and none in git history.**

So `plan_/apply_ufcstats_history_merge.py` is a workaround for the missing half,
and it copies **dev to prod** while the only live ingest writes **prod**. The
planner now reports `rows that would insert: 0` and dev sits at 194 against
prod's 199. Dev can only fall further behind.

`settlement/ufc_settle.py`, `routers/props.py` and `routers/games/ufc.py` all

features degrade silently.

Codex's 71-row apply, interrupted at its usage limit, is **not** outstanding:
