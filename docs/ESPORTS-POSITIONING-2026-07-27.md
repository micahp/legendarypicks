## The finding

Research question: has any major, top-tier esports tournament been carried **live** on
mainstream US linear TV (ABC, ESPN/ESPN2, TBS, FS1, CBS Sports Network) in the five years
from 2021-07-26 to 2026-07-26?

**No.** Several things look like it and fall apart on inspection:

| Event | Year | US distribution | Live on linear TV? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ESL Pro Tour / IEM Katowice | 2021–22 | G4 | **No.** Prime-time programs built from "action and cut downs" across eight competitions |
| G4 Gameday LCS | 2022 | G4, Pluto TV | **No.** Weekly highlights, analysis, postgame interviews; matches stayed on LoL Esports/Twitch/YouTube |
| Ultimate Madden Bowl | 2022 | ESPN, ESPN2 | **No.** Live on Twitch/YouTube/ESPN App Feb 8; TV "premiere" Feb 11 |
| Rocket League X Games Open | 2022 | ESPN App, Twitch, YouTube | Live, but **not linear** |
| BLAST Austin Major | 2025 | BLAST.tv, Twitch, YouTube | Online only in the US |
| Esports World Cup Spotlight | 2025 | Fox Sports | **No.** Produced series, not live matches |

The last clean counter-example is the **2021 eNASCAR iRacing Pro Invitational Series** — five
virtual races live in prime time on FS1 (Mar 24 Bristol Dirt, Apr 21 Talladega, May 5
Darlington, May 19 COTA, Jun 2 Chicago). Just outside a literal five-year window, and a
hybrid: real NASCAR drivers in iRacing, not full-time esports pros.

The earlier internal answer on this was **too absolute in its wording, but correct in its
conclusion.**

## What it means for us

This makes the opportunity better, but **do not frame the product around "esports on TV."**

Traditional sports data products organize everything around television networks. Esports
distribution is fragmented across publisher streams, league sites, Twitch, YouTube,
co-streamers, DAZN, and small linear services like ESTV (which runs 24/7 esports channels on
connected-TV platforms — a different animal from an ESPN or TBS broadcast).

**The framing:**

> Every live competition you can legally watch right now, regardless of whether it is on TV,
> YouTube, Twitch, DAZN or a free smart-TV channel.

Normalized into one interface:

```
LIVE NOW
COD League • OpTic vs. FaZe • Map 3
  → Watch free on YouTube
  • current series score        • live map score
  • pre-match projection        • win probability
  • player kills vs projection  • momentum / objective control
  • "what just happened?" summary
```

**The distinction, in one line:**

> Legendary Picks makes internet-native sports feel as trackable as an NBA game on ESPN.

The scarcity of real TV coverage is the point: there is no incumbent ESPN-style esports
scoreboard to displace. The streams exist, the audiences exist, the matches exist. The
missing piece is the unified **live data and discovery layer**.
