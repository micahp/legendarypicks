## The NHL pick record (committed as `15752d75`)

A code review of the NHL Model tab (read from GitHub, not the live site) raised six findings. All six were checked
against `dev` and all six hold. Two were worked today.

**The defect.** The pick was computed in the browser on every page load from the newest Bovada line, while the
forecast row was immutable. Two consequences. The recommendation could change between visits without the forecast
changing, and the Record tab never graded the recommendation at all: `RecordProjection` picked by
`home_win_probability >= 0.5`, a moneyline, while the card could show a puck line or a total. The record graded a
different selection from the one a reader was shown.

**What changed.**
- `backend/nhl_model_store.py`. New table `nhl_model_picks`, one immutable row per game and model, foreign keyed to
  the projection row, with update and delete triggers that abort. Columns: market, side, line, price,
  model_probability, market_probability, edge, expected_value, line_source, line_captured_at_utc,
  settlement_basis, provenance, selected_at_utc, pick_sha256.
- The pick rule moved out of the browser into `model_pick()` in the same module, ported exactly, including the
  `Number(x.toFixed(1))` comparison that rounds halves away from zero. There is now one implementation, so the card,
  the record and the stored row cannot drift apart.
- `lock_feed` freezes each pick against `latest_line_at(con, gid, now)`, the Bovada quote in force at that instant.
  `grade_pick` grades from the stored row under the `settlement_basis` it recorded. `export_locked` attaches the pick
  and its result; `export_previews` derives a display-only pick with the same function and never stores it.
- `backend/backfill_nhl_picks.py` (new). Freezes picks for lock rows that predate the record, priced from the line at
  their own lock timestamp. Dry run by default, `--apply` to write, refuses a Legendary Picks database path.
- `backend/routers/nhl_model.py`. The market snapshot is read once and passed to both the locked and preview exports.
- `components/Leagues/NhlModelTab.tsx`. The client-side `modelPick`, `getPickResult` and no-vig math are deleted, about
  90 lines. Cards and the record render `forecast.pick`. The record shows the pick label with its model probability,
  the result, and the frozen quote time and price.

**Verification, measured.**
- `backend/test_nhl_model_locks.py`: 23 tests, 6 new, all pass under `backend/venv/bin/python`. Two tests fail and
  both failed before this work started: `test_forecast_after_start_and_wrong_lock_window_fail_closed`
  (`(3,0,0) != (0,1,3)`) and `test_applied_daily_grade_run_also_refreshes_upcoming_previews` (`48 != 6`, the fixture
  still expects two games while the committed ledger holds sixteen). Both are recorded here so the next person does
  not read them as new.
- `components/Leagues/NhlModelTab.test.tsx`: 5 of 5 pass. The three tests that used to exercise the rule in the
  browser now render a pick supplied by the payload; the rule itself is tested in Python.
- End to end against a copy of `backend/data/nhl-model.dev.sqlite3`, through `locks_payload`: 48 picks across 16
  locked games, none missing. By market: 22 spread, 17 moneyline, 9 total. 36 graded, 28 won and 8 lost. Inserting a
  line captured in 2099 moved 0 picks.
- **9 of the 22 graded non-moneyline picks have a result that differs from the moneyline the old record graded.**
  The record was answering a different question from the card about 41 percent of the time on those picks. That is
  the number this work changes.
- The live endpoint answers on `:3096` and `:8096` and serves the picks.

**One defect introduced and corrected the same session.** The first cut also re-priced already-locked rows, so a
lock run could freeze a pick from a line posted after the forecast locked. The installed lock timer (every 15
minutes) picked that up within minutes and wrote 9 such rows for three games locked between 01:15 and 17:15 UTC,
priced from an 08:40 UTC line. The branch was removed, so `lock_feed` now freezes only the row it locks in that run
and leaves legacy rows to the backfill. The 9 rows were then deleted and rebuilt from their own lock-time line. The
signature used to find them is `selected_at_utc != locked_at_utc`. After the correction: 48 rows, every row with
`selected_at == locked_at`, both triggers present, immutability confirmed enforced.

## NFL player projections: the served baseline, calibration, and full Marcel

The v2 player engine had never been graded against anything, and the probability the site actually serves had
never been graded at all. Three questions were answered today, all read-only against `picks.dev.db`, all on
`scratch/spec-b-rebase`. Nothing here is on `dev` and nothing is served.

**The served baseline.** `backend/grade_nfl_prop_baseline.py` (new) grades `analytics/projections.prob_over`,
the share of a player's own same-season games that beat the line. It imports its item set from the
pre-registered v2 grader, so the lines, books, devig, weeks and eligible player-games are identical code. On
2026 weeks 4 and 5, the only weeks the route can serve because `project_stat` returns nothing below three
games: 14,431 items on 354 player-games, Brier 0.3082 against the market's 0.2463, which is worse than a
constant 0.5 (0.2500). Of the 33,519 items v2 grades, 19,088 (57%) have no live number at all. Replaying the
route as served, target game included, gives 0.1911: that is look-ahead, not accuracy.

**Calibration is nearly the whole measured gap, and it costs two parameters.**
`backend/calibrate_nfl_prop_probabilities.py` (new) fits a probability map on other weeks and scores the
held-out week, leave-one-week-out over weeks 2 to 5 on 33,519 items. A two-parameter logistic recalibration
takes v2 from 0.2747 to 0.2488 against the market's 0.2460, interval excluding zero. Murphy decomposition:
reliability falls 0.0260 to 0.0007 while resolution barely moves, 0.0016 to 0.0019 against the market's
0.0034. The entire excess over the floor was miscalibration, and the repair works by shrinking toward 0.5
rather than by adding information: after it the model still disagrees with the market on 31% of items and hits
42.5% of those sides. Per-market isotonic overfits the 122-player-game markets; pooled Platt is the robust
choice. No new data was needed for any of this.

**Full Marcel is not the upgrade it looks like.** `backend/marcel_nfl_backtest.py` (new) implements the 5/4/3
multi-season weighting, regression to the position mean and an aging curve, fitted on 2013 to 2021 and graded
walk-forward on 2025. Receiving yards MAE over 2,990 identical player-games runs 19.494 at window 0, 19.579 at
1, 19.780 at 2, 19.928 at 3 and 20.013 at 5. **Every added season makes the point estimate worse,
monotonically.** Regression is the only component that pays; the aging curve hurts (20.446 at window 3).
Marcel-lite itself scores 20.163, so the best configuration measured here beats what the site serves. For the
probability the opposite holds: Brier 0.3064 at window 0 to 0.2658 at window 3, and the first prior season is
worth 0.0378 of that 0.0406. Window 0 reproduces marcel-lite's 0.3082, which is the control that validates the
harness.

**The ladder, paired on 31,843 identical items on 838 player-games:** market 0.2461, v2 plus Platt 0.2492,
full Marcel window 3 0.2666, v2 raw 0.2779. Calibration minus raw v2 is +0.02873 +/- 0.00967; Marcel minus raw
v2 is +0.01134 +/- 0.01193, which spans zero and is therefore not established; calibration minus Marcel is
+0.01739 +/- 0.00936. Marcel's log loss is worse than v2's, 0.7836 against 0.7618, despite the better Brier,
because an empirical hit rate makes confident wrong calls.

Full detail in `docs/RESULTS-nfl-prop-baseline-2026-10-16.md` and
`docs/RESULTS-nfl-marcel-and-calibration-2026-10-16.md` on `scratch/spec-b-rebase`, which also carries the four
new scripts. The two commits are `491c4fc2` and `89a80841`, pushed.

## Process notes

- The pick rule now lives in one place. Two implementations of it, one in the browser and one on the server, is the
  shape of the defect this session fixed. Do not reintroduce a client-side copy for previews or anything else.
- A row that is immutable and a row that is correct are different claims. The first cut produced 9 immutable rows that
  were wrong, and only a check against `locked_at_utc` found them. Check provenance, not just presence.
- The lock timer runs every 15 minutes and picks up backend edits within that window, so a change to the lock path is
  live almost immediately on dev. Verify on a copy first.
- A unit file in the repo is documentation until someone diffs it against `systemctl cat`. Both NHL timers had drifted
  from what was running and the repo versions described a product that did not exist. Diff all six files after any
  change to a scheduled job, and treat the running unit as the source of truth until someone decides otherwise.
- **A Brier score on its own can hide a model that says nothing.** Decompose before claiming an improvement:
  v2's entire excess over the coin-flip floor was reliability, 0.0260, and its resolution was 0.0016 against the
  market's 0.0034. A recalibration that shrinks everything toward 0.5 scores well while adding no information, and
  the way to see that is resolution and the spread of the predictions, not the headline number.
- **More history is not automatically better, and the direction differs by target.** For per-game NFL stats the
  added seasons made the point estimate worse at every step while making the probability better, because the
  probability gain comes from the sample size in the denominator rather than from better information about the
  player. Decide per target, not per model.
- **Check whether the promotion gate, not the data, is what blocks a fix.** The player gate is mean absolute error
  only, so a change that improves the probability while raising MAE cannot be adopted however good it is. When a
  measured weakness has an obvious fix that keeps being rejected, read the gate before commissioning more data.
- Copy rules stand: no word "bet", no em dashes, no status tags on public pages. This document follows them.
