# COHORT — does the direct-props CRPS advantage survive on active players?

**Verdict: no.** The `ADOPT_CANDIDATE` verdicts in `RESULTS.md` hold only in
aggregate, and the aggregate is carried by the rows where the athlete recorded
nothing. On the athletes who actually record the stat — and on regular
participants, and on high-volume players — the model is **worse than the
trailing-5 baseline on CRPS in all four markets**, and under-predicts them by
roughly 25%.

Generated by `cohort_analysis.py` from the committed `predictions_<market>.csv`
(2025 out-of-sample rows only). Machine output: `cohort_2025.csv`,
`cohort_detail.json`. `delta` = `CRPS_model − CRPS_baseA`; **negative = model
better**. CIs are 90%, bootstrapped on `game_id` (10,000 resamples, seed
20261020), the same rule as `verdict_2025_clustered.csv`.

The sign flips cleanly: every aggregate win is negative, every active-player
cohort is positive, and no active cohort satisfies the ADOPT rule. The
`regular` and `top_volume` cuts rule out the objection that `in_game` just
catches backup cameos — the athletes a user would look up are the ones the
model is worst for.

## Where the aggregate win actually comes from

Rows where the athlete recorded nothing are 72–74% of every market. The model
predicts near-zero for them (mean 0.8–11.5) while baseA predicts a shrunk but
nonzero mean; CRPS heavily rewards the model there, and that reward is larger
than the entire reported advantage.

| market | total ΔA | contribution from `in_game` | contribution from `zero_usage` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pass attempts | −0.324 | **+0.278** (worse) | −0.602 |

| rushing carries | −0.158 | **+0.112** (worse) | −0.270 |
| rushing yards | −1.582 | **+0.410** (worse) | −1.993 |

The active rows contribute a *positive* (harmful) delta in every market; the
zeros contribute a negative delta larger in magnitude than the headline. The
"four candidates beat the baselines" result is real arithmetic and useless
product: it says the model is good at saying "this backup will not play."

## Mechanism

1. **Trailing mean is biased low for active games.** `_tmean` averages the
   athlete's last 5 *box-score appearances*, which include zero-usage cameos, so
   the conditioning is on appearance, not on usage. Even the raw trailing mean
   (baseA) predicts 16.8 attempts for rows that observed 21.0.
2. **The model shrinks it further.** `base = (n·raw + k·pm)/(n+k)` with tuned
   `k=0.25` shrinks toward a position mean fitted over *all* appearances
   (including zeros). Net for active QBs: model 15.4 < baseA 16.8 < observed
   21.0 — a ~27% under-prediction.
3. **Under-dispersion compounds it.** `sd_ratio` 0.49–0.76 (from
   `decomposition_2025.csv`) and active-cohort `cov90` of 0.70–0.82 against
   nominal 0.90 make the intervals too narrow exactly where the outcome is
   spread out.

So it is not a scoring artifact or a cohort-definition trick: on active rows the
model has a worse mean (higher MAE) *and* a worse spread (higher CRPS, low
cov90) than a naive trailing-5 baseline.

## Consequences for the plan

- **Do not ship the proposed "Player Projections" page off these four models.**
  The users of that page are active players, and that is the population the
  model is worst for. The aggregate CRPS number would be an honest statistic and
  a dishonest product claim.
- The fix is the two-part / hurdle structure already listed as step 3, now with
  a concrete reason: model participation and expected usage separately from
  conditional-on-usage volume, and report the **conditional** distribution for
  players expected to play. Refit the trailing mean conditional on
  usage (`observed > 0`) or usage-weight it.
- Prospective locking (step 4) must lock the **conditional-on-active**
  distribution; locking the unconditional one would freeze the same defect.
- The ADOPT_CANDIDATE label in `RESULTS.md` should be read as "beats baselines
  on the all-rows CRPS under the registered rule," not "useful player
  projection." The registered rule was never conditioned on the population that
  matters.
