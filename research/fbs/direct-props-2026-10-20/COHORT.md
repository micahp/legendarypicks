# COHORT — does the direct-props CRPS advantage survive on active players?

**Headline (revised after adding the pregame-definable cohort).** The aggregate
`ADOPT_CANDIDATE` verdicts are carried by the zero-usage rows: on rows where the
athlete actually records the stat, the model is worse than the trailing-5
baseline in all four markets. But the *pregame-definable* cohort — the only one
that can carry a product claim — tells a **market-specific** story:

| market | ΔA on `pregame_known` [90% CI] | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| rushing carries | **−0.124** [−0.162, −0.087] | beats both baselines → ADOPT holds |
| rushing yards | **−1.923** [−2.304, −1.556] | beats both baselines → ADOPT holds |
| passing attempts | **+0.144** [+0.028, +0.261] | beaten by trailing-5 → no product claim |
| passing yards | **+4.014** [+2.363, +5.665] | beaten by trailing-5 → no product claim |

So the advantage **survives for rushing and fails for passing** on the athletes a
user can be shown before kickoff. The earlier blanket wording ("the athletes a
user would look up are the ones the model is worst for") is true for passing and
**too strong for rushing**; this file replaces it.

Generated by `cohort_analysis.py` from the committed `predictions_<market>.csv`
(2025 out-of-sample rows) plus the read-only Phase-5 cache for 2024 volume.
Machine output: `cohort_2025.csv`, `cohort_detail.json`. `delta` =
`CRPS_model − CRPS_baseA`; **negative = model better**. CIs are 90%,
bootstrapped on `game_id` (10,000 resamples, seed 20261020).

## What each cohort can and cannot support

- **`in_game` / `zero_usage`** — a **decomposition** of the aggregate CRPS. They
  say where the win comes from; they do **not** say what the model is worth to a
  user.
- **`regular_posthoc` / `top_volume_posthoc`** — **post-hoc, outcome-aware**
  cuts (membership uses full-season information, including the target game and
  later weeks). Directional evidence only; **not** pregame-definable.
- **`pregame_known`** — the only cohort defined from information available
  before kickoff, evaluated over **all** rows of qualifying athletes (zeros
  included). **The only cohort that can carry a product claim.**

Against the **strong** baseline (trailing-5) the model loses on both passing
markets and wins on both rushing markets. Against the weak position-league

- **Rushing projections can be defended** on a pregame cohort: the CRPS win is
  real and the intervals are close to nominal (cov90 0.912 / 0.935).
- **Passing projections cannot**: a plain trailing-5 baseline beats the model on
  exactly the starting QBs a projections page would show.

## Where the aggregate win actually comes from

Zeros are the majority of every market, and the model scores far better on them
(it predicts near-zero where baseA predicts a nonzero shrunk mean):

| market | total ΔA | contribution from `in_game` | contribution from `zero_usage` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pass attempts | −0.324 | **+0.278** (worse) | −0.602 |
| passing yards | −2.881 | **+2.803** (worse) | −5.654 |
| rushing carries | −0.158 | **+0.112** (worse) | −0.270 |
| rushing yards | −1.582 | **+0.410** (worse) | −1.901 |

The active rows contribute a *positive* (harmful) delta in every market. The
aggregate `ADOPT_CANDIDATE` is a statement about zero-usage rows.

## Mechanism

1. **Trailing mean is biased low for active games.** `_tmean` averages the
   athlete's last 5 *box-score appearances*, which include zero-usage cameos, so
   it is conditioned on appearance, not usage. Even the raw trailing mean
   (baseA) predicts 16.8 attempts for rows that observed 21.0.
2. **The model shrinks it further.** `base = (n·raw + k·pm)/(n+k)` with tuned
   `k=0.25` shrinks toward a position mean fitted over *all* appearances
   (including zeros). For active QBs: model 15.4 < baseA 16.8 < observed 21.0
   (~27% under-prediction). This is why passing fails on `pregame_known`.
3. **Under-dispersion compounds it.** `sd_ratio` 0.49–0.76 (from
   `decomposition_2025.csv`) and active-cohort `cov90` of 0.70–0.82 against
   nominal 0.90 make the intervals too narrow exactly where the outcome is
   spread out.

Rushing survives on `pregame_known` because top rushers still post occasional
zero-carry games (injury, blowouts) that the model predicts well, and their
per-active-game bias (+0.33 carries) is small; passing has almost no zero-attempt
starting games and a large active-game bias, so nothing offsets the loss.

## Consequences for the plan

- **Passing: do not ship a projections page** off this model. On the
  pregame-definable starting-QB cohort it is beaten by trailing-5 on CRPS,
  cov90 0.81, and ~27% low on active-game volume.
- **Rushing: defensible** on the pregame cohort (adopt holds vs both baselines),
  with the caveat that the in-game rows are still biased low; the claim is about
  the unconditional "will he produce" distribution, not per-active-game volume.
- The real fix for passing is the two-part / hurdle structure (step 3): model
  participation and usage separately from conditional-on-usage volume, refit the
  trailing mean conditional on `observed > 0`, and widen dispersion. Prospective
  locking (step 4) must lock the **conditional-on-active** distribution.
- The ADOPT_CANDIDATE label in `RESULTS.md` should be read as "beats baselines
  on the all-rows CRPS under the registered rule." On the pregame cohort that
  label survives for rushing and not for passing.
